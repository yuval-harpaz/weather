MW is a megawatt, a million watts — a rate of power, not an amount of energy. It is how much the whole country is drawing at that instant. The peak here is the highest such moment of the day: on a hot August afternoon in 2025 it reached 17,270 MW, or 17.3 GW, roughly seventeen million kettles at once. Reserve is the generation that was available and not being used at that moment — the headroom. Quoted as a percentage it is that headroom as a share of the peak, so 5% means the country was running with one part spare for every twenty in use.

Source: Peak-hour data published by Noga, the Israel Independent System Operator, under its reporting obligation, one file per month.
